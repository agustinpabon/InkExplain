# Stickman Identity

Stickman is one recurring stick figure, drawn the same way in every image of a series: a person reduced to a few confident strokes, whose whole body does the explaining. It is a character, not a pictogram. Its head, face, proportions, and way of standing are fixed, so a reader recognizes it while it carries, pulls, sorts, bridges, and weighs.

## Identity

Draw it from this contract. Proportions are in head diameters (H); a standing figure is 5H tall.

- **Head**: an open round head, one outlined circle that is never filled, 1H across. It is drawn in a single loop whose ends overlap slightly, so it is a little imperfect, never a compass circle.
- **Face**: two dot eyes, small and solid, set just below the head's midline and turned toward the action. At most two short brow strokes show focus, doubt, or surprise. No mouth, nose, ears, or hair. Expression comes from the brows, the head tilt, and the body.
- **Spine**: one curved spine line from the base of the head to the hips, 1.6H long, leaning toward the action. It is never ruler-straight and never perfectly vertical.
- **Limbs**: two-segment limbs. Each arm is 1.0H + 1.0H joined at a soft elbow and attached 0.25H below the head; each leg is 1.2H + 1.2H joined at a soft knee and attached at the hips. Hanging arms reach about mid-thigh, and the legs are longer than the spine.
- **Hands**: none. Each arm ends in a plain rounded stroke end that touches or overlaps the object it works on; the arm line itself holds the object.
- **Feet**: foot ticks, one short stroke (about 0.35H) at the end of each leg, pointing the way the figure faces, so stance and direction read at a glance.
- **Posture**: asymmetrical, with the weight on one leg and the spine leaning into the action. Never a stiff T-pose, never a mirrored stance, and never standing beside the action.
- **Size**: the standing figure is about one third of the canvas height, larger when it bends or reaches, never smaller than one fifth.
- **Line**: one marker weight, about H/9 thick, a little heavier than the props. See `style.md`.

## Recognition

Markers: open round head, two dot eyes, curved spine line, two-segment limbs, foot ticks.

- Keep at least three across transformations, and all five in final images.
- In a series the head stays the same size relative to the body, the line weight stays the same, and the face stays the same: dots, with brows only when the expression needs them.
- Two figures appear only to compare two states, and then both are the same Stickman drawn the same way, differing only in pose and expression.
- Whatever else changes (the props, the pose, the metaphor, the accent), the proportions do not.

## Failure modes

- The figure becomes a pictogram or mannequin: solid limbs, a block torso, a triangle dress, a symmetrical sign-like stance.
- A stiff T-pose, a mirrored stance, or a figure standing beside the action while the objects do the work.
- An emoji or cartoon face: a mouth, smile, blush, ears, big glossy eyes, or hair.
- A children's activity-book look: cute round proportions, an oversized head, colored limbs.
- A polished corporate vector figure: uniform line width, a perfect circle, gradients, a drop shadow.
- Hands, fingers, mittens, shoes, clothes, a hat, or any accessory on the figure.
- Proportion drift: a chibi head, stubby limbs, a spine that grows or shrinks, a filled head.
- More than one figure when the idea needs one, or two figures that differ in design.
- A name tag, logo, or label on the figure.

## Participation test

Cover the figure with your thumb. If the image still explains the idea, the figure is decoration: give it the decisive action.
