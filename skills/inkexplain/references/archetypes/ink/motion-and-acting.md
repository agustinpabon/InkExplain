# InkExplain Sketch Motion and Acting

How the actor moves, and how the shared structure types from `references/workflow/composition-grid.md` look in InkExplain Sketch.

## Actions

Carry, sort, weigh, pull a lever, open a gate, patch a leak, bridge a gap, filter, stack, untangle, label, measure, guard, hand over, wind a crank, sweep, plant, fold, unpack, connect.

Choose the verb that is the idea. "Trust is built step by step" becomes the ink figure laying stones across a gap; "too many inputs" becomes two hands holding back an overflowing funnel.

### Poses and acting

- Put the whole body into the action: the ink figure leans into a load, braces against a lever, or reaches across the gap the idea crosses.
- Show weight and direction with the single-stroke body: a bent line pushes, a straight line stands guard, an arc reaches.
- Hands enter from the frame edge and touch the object they place, hold, or adjust; they never float free.
- Add two or three short motion marks in explainer blue when movement matters, never more.

### Timing

- A single image shows the decisive moment: the instant the lever moves or the last stone lands, not the setup or the aftermath.
- Mini comics use two to four beats (setup, action, result), one action per beat, with the actor drawn the same way in every panel.

## Patterns

- **Workflow**: material enters on the left as loose scribbles, passes through one hand-built machine worked by the ink figure, and leaves on the right as a few clean shapes. One explainer blue arrow carries the flow.
- **System close-up**: draw only the decisive part, large, with the ink figure working inside it. Suggest the rest of the system with two or three faint outlines.
- **Before and after**: a tangle on the left, the same material in order on the right, and the ink figure making the change in the middle.
- **State change**: two to four small ink figures in different postures, one short label each; the state that matters gets the highlighter swipe.
- **Concept metaphor**: one large, slightly absurd object, such as a scale weighing two clouds or a funnel the size of a room, worked by the ink figure.
- **Layers**: a hand-drawn stack with slightly uneven layers; the ink figure props up, inspects, or adds one.
- **Route map**: one wandering explainer blue path with three to five stops; the ink figure walks it or carries something along it.
- **Mini comic**: two to four loose panels separated by whitespace instead of hard frames, one action per panel.

## Supporting objects

Everyday, low-tech things: pipes, valves, funnels, sieves, scales, drawers, shelves, boxes, jars, keys, locks, gates, bridges, ladders, ropes, pulleys, levers, cranks, lamps, maps, signposts, stepping stones, envelopes, stamps, buckets, nets, and clotheslines.

Use one or two per image, and prefer objects with obvious physical behavior: things that fill, leak, balance, open, jam, or connect.

## Originality

- Invent a new metaphor for each source; do not reuse a scene from earlier images.
- Two images in one series never share both the structure type and the main object.
- When an idea repeats across articles, keep the idea and change the object and the action.
