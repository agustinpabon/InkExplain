# InkExplain Sketch Style

The house style of InkExplain: an explainer's sketch on clean paper. Black ink for everything, one blue pen for movement, and a single stroke of highlighter on what matters most.

## Canvas

- 16:9 landscape by default; follow the user's aspect ratio when they ask for another.
- A clean white background with no paper texture, grain, gradient, vignette, or shadow.
- One focal action, roughly in the middle third (a state change or a comparison spreads its states across the canvas), and at least 35% of the canvas left empty.

## Linework

- Hand-drawn black ink: medium-thin lines with a slight natural wobble and rounded ends.
- One consistent stroke weight across a whole series; a slightly heavier stroke only for the actor's outline.
- Sparse hatching for shade. No fills other than the accents below.

## Color

The palette is fixed and restrained: at least 85% of every image is black ink on white.

| Role | Color | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Ink | near-black `#1F1F1F` | outlines, objects, and labels |
| Explainer blue | `#2F6FED` | movement only: arrows, paths, flow lines, motion marks |
| Highlighter yellow | translucent `#FFD84D` | one loose highlighter swipe behind the single most important label or object |
| Signal red | `#E5484D` | only a problem, warning, or breakpoint; one or two marks at most |

- Use highlighter yellow once per image, twice at most in a side-by-side comparison.
- Never use color for decoration or to fill large areas.

## Labels

- Two to six short labels in neat handwritten print, in black ink; labels on a flow may be explainer blue.
- The key label sits on the highlighter yellow swipe.
- Copy labels exactly in the user's language, one to three words each.
- No title, caption, legend, or paragraph.

## Avoid

- Mascots, logos, brand marks, and recognizable characters from any franchise.
- Slides, infographics, flowcharts, dashboards, UI, code, or terminal screens.
- Glossy vector art, 3D, photorealism, or a stock-illustration look.
- Cute cartoon, chibi, or children's-book treatment.
- Textured or tinted paper, watercolor washes, gradients, or drop shadows.
- Dense diagrams with more than six labels or more than one main arrow.
