# InkExplain Sketch Identity

InkExplain Sketch has no mascot. The actor is whatever performs the idea most clearly, drawn in the same ink as everything else.

## Identity

Choose one actor per image, and keep the choice consistent across a series when the ideas allow it:

- **The ink figure** (default): a minimal, anonymous person with a round head, a single-stroke body, and thin limbs. No face, or two small dot eyes at most; no hair, clothing details, or age and gender cues. It stands about a fifth of the canvas tall.
- **Hands**: one or two hand-drawn hands entering from the edge of the frame, for ideas about choosing, holding, placing, or adjusting.
- **The working object**: the machine, scale, or gate acts by itself (gears turn, a lid snaps shut) when no person is needed.

The ink figure is a pen, not a personality: neutral, calm, and competent. It may strain, stumble, or stall when the idea is a failure, as long as its construction stays the same.

## Series lock

- **Locked** in a series: the kind of actor you chose (the ink figure, hands, or the working object) while the ideas allow it; for the ink figure, the round head, the single-stroke body, the thin limbs, and the same proportions and stroke weight; the roles of ink, blue, yellow, and red in `style.md`.
- **Free**: the figure's pose, position, and size, what it holds, and the scene around it.
- **Rejected**: a face beyond two dot eyes, hair, clothes, a name, or a recurring prop on the figure, and proportions or stroke weight that change from one image to the next.

## Recognition

- The ink figure keeps the same proportions and stroke weight in every image of a series: round head, single-stroke body, thin limbs.
- Hands match the scene's line style, with no nail detail, rings, or branded sleeves.
- Whatever acts sits at the center of the action, never in a corner.

## Failure modes

- The ink figure turns into a character: facial features, hair, clothes, a name, or a recurring prop.
- A recognizable franchise character or brand appears.
- Several figures crowd a scene that needs one. Only a state change or a comparison shows the same figure more than once, identical in each state.
- The figure is drawn stiffly, like a diagram glyph instead of a person in motion.
- The actor watches while the objects do the work.

## Participation test

Cover the actor with your thumb. If the image still explains the idea, the actor is decoration: give it the decisive action.
