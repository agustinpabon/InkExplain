# Tux Identity

Tux, the Linux penguin created by Larry Ewing, is the actor in every image of this archetype: a seated, rounded penguin who explains review, sorting, connection, boundary setting, comparison, repair, decision gates, sequencing, trust, source context, and handoffs by doing the work.

## Identity

Preserve all eight markers together. No reference image is bundled; this list is the identity authority (see `rights.md`).

- glossy black rounded penguin head and body
- white face eye patches
- large oval eyes with dark pupils and small highlights
- yellow-orange beak with two nostril dots
- white oval belly
- long black flippers
- seated rounded posture
- oversized yellow-orange webbed feet

## Series lock

- **Locked** in a series: all eight markers: the glossy black rounded head and body, the white face eye patches, the large oval eyes with dark pupils and small highlights, the yellow-orange beak with two nostril dots, the white oval belly, the long black flippers, the seated rounded posture, and the oversized yellow-orange webbed feet. No reference image is bundled, so the written list is the whole lock: copy the Character paragraph unchanged into every prompt.
- **Free**: how the seated body turns toward the work, what the flippers do, its scale, and the objects it handles.
- **Rejected**: a marker lost or replaced, a generic penguin, and the static logo pose.

## Recognition

- All eight markers read clearly at article-sketch simplicity, so the character is Tux, not a penguin in general.
- Tux acts inside the metaphor, never beside it as a spectator.
- Each scene gets a fresh pose built around the action, never the familiar frontal, idle logo pose.

## Boundaries

- Selection: only this archetype's aliases select it (Tux, Linux Mascot, Linux, Linux penguin, Tux penguin), and only when the user asks for Tux as the illustration character. An article about Linux does not select it. Broad terms never do: penguin, server, kernel, distro, distro logo, Linux Foundation, operating system, CLI, terminal, brand campaign, mascot.
- Tux is the only mascot in the frame: no distro mascots, and nothing from other archetypes.
- Rights and restrictions live in `rights.md`; the rights note in `prompts.md` carries them into every prompt.

## Failure modes

- Generic penguin or server-mascot drift: a naturalistic penguin, plush toy, generic tech or server mascot, a different mascot, an over-rendered toy, or a vague black-and-white silhouette.
- Missing markers: any of the eight identity markers is lost or replaced.
- Distro-logo drift: distro logos, distro mascots, distro badges, distro branding, package-manager marks, or Linux Foundation logos appear, or stand in for Tux's identity.
- Endorsement claims: images, labels, captions, prompts, or reports imply official Linux, Linux Foundation, or distro endorsement, affiliation, sponsorship, approval, certification, or compatibility, or use Linux Foundation campaign framing.
- Product-output drift: a product poster, CLI screenshot, kernel dashboard, web hero graphic, or operating-system marketing graphic replaces the article sketch.
- Passive placement: Tux is tiny, decorative, removable, or only reacting, or sits beside the concept while props, arrows, or labels carry the meaning.
- Copied pose: the familiar static logo pose, or a pose traced from any reference image, instead of a pose that performs the action.

## Participation test

Cover Tux with your thumb. If the image still explains the idea, Tux is decoration: rewrite the prompt so Tux performs the decisive action.
