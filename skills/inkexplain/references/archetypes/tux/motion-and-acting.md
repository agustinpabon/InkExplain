# Tux Motion and Acting

How Tux moves, and how the shared structure types from `references/workflow/composition-grid.md` look with Tux: one core idea, one physical metaphor, and one active Tux action per image.

## Actions

Tux performs the central cognitive action of every image. Tie eye direction, beak direction, flipper position, body direction, seated posture, and feet to that action.

- **Point** a flipper at the useful turn, risky gap, stable claim, or next action.
- **Inspect** a map, source card, lens, thread, gate, or evidence shelf.
- **Carry** a key, envelope, lamp, stamp, map piece, source card, review tag, or bridge part.
- **Sort** envelopes, cards, buckets, labels, paths, or small evidence pieces.
- **Bridge** two stepping stones, shelves, paths, article sections, or trust gaps.
- **Repair** a broken bridge, tangled line, loose hinge, trail marker, or small hand-built machine.
- **Guide** a reader through a trail, map, gate, or signpost sequence.
- **Map, mark, or redirect** the decisive point, hidden path, dependency, sequence, signal, claim, or handoff.
- **Stamp** a reviewed idea, checkpoint, source note, or handoff.
- **Shield** a fragile idea, boundary, source note, or decision.
- **Weigh** two claims, tradeoffs, paths, or choices on scales.
- **Compare** two trails, gates, buckets, shelves, or labels.
- **Connect or untangle** threads, trails, shelves, handoffs, stepping stones, knots, or competing claims.
- **Lift** a blocked idea, heavy claim, gate, or bridge piece.
- **Assemble or tune** a small machine, shelf, bridge, trail, review ladder, scale, lamp, compass, signal, or lever.

Useful verbs: point, inspect, carry, sort, bridge, repair, guide, map, mark, redirect, stamp, shield, weigh, compare, connect, untangle, lift, assemble, tune. The action always serves the core idea.

### Poses and acting

- Body language: focused, inspecting, pointing, carrying, sorting, bridging, repairing, guiding, stamping, marking, tuning, shielding, weighing, connecting, untangling, mapping, comparing, lifting, assembling, or redirecting.
- Keep the seated, rounded posture as the base and act from it: the whole rounded body turns toward the object Tux works on.
- Aim the eyes and beak at the work, put a long black flipper on the object (pointing, pressing, holding, or stamping), and set the oversized feet so they brace or carry the action.
- Build a fresh pose for every scene around the action; never fall back to the familiar frontal, idle logo pose.

### Timing

- A single image shows the decisive moment: the stamp landing on the checkpoint, the flipper setting the last stepping stone, the knot coming loose, not the setup or the aftermath.
- Mini comics use two or three beats, one Tux action each, with Tux identical across panels.

## Patterns

- **Workflow** (input-to-output reasoning, source review, editorial handoffs, learning sequences, decision paths, repair flows, method steps): a loose left-to-right or curved path. Tux operates the decisive handoff point: points to a turn, carries a key, sorts envelopes, bridges stepping stones, repairs a broken link, stamps a checkpoint, or redirects a signal along the path.
- **System close-up** (filters, decision points, hidden dependencies, evidence clusters, priority shelves, comparison layers, resilience checks): only three to five core parts. Tux works inside the decisive one: inspects a lens, untangles threads, weighs claims on scales, shields a source note, tunes a lamp, repairs a lever, or connects two shelves.
- **Before and after** (vague to clear, tangled to readable, scattered to sorted, fragile to stable, overloaded to focused, stuck to moving): unstable on the left, calmer on the right. Tux performs the change in the center with a knot, bridge, map, gate, shelf, scale, stamp, bucket, trail marker, source card, or small hand-built machine.
- **State change** (focused review, careful comparison, overloaded sorting, guarded judgment, stuck reasoning, confident handoff): two to four compact Tux states, each with one short label and one physical action.
- **Concept metaphor** (judgment scales, trust bridges, decision gates, tangled threads, map trails, evidence shelves, review lamps, hidden keys, signal switches, source cards): one memorable low-tech object or small hand-built machine, with Tux as its operator.
- **Layers** (layered methods, capability stacks, reasoning ladders, evidence shelves, review depth, confidence building, staged handoffs): stacked boxes, shelves, steps, or loose construction layers. Tux carries, shields, stamps, sorts, tunes, repairs, weighs, bridges, lifts, or assembles one layer so the method becomes usable.
- **Route map** (idea paths, reader journeys, migration paths, decision trails, source trails, problem-resolution paths): one curved path with a few nodes. Tux walks, guides, bridges, reroutes, repairs, stamps, marks, compares, maps, or carries along the decisive segment.
- **Mini comic** (failure-to-fix sequences, repeated reasoning mistakes, handoff stories, comparison lessons, small process stories): two or three loose panels, one Tux action per panel, with Tux identical across panels.

## Supporting objects

Small physical objects that make article logic visible:

- maps, folded maps, dotted paths, trails, trail markers, paper paths, forked paths, and signposts;
- stepping stones, bridges, gates, shelves, ladders, and handoff boards;
- knots, threads, ropes, hooks, clips, and loose wires;
- lamps, lenses, compasses, scales, stamps, keys, buckets, envelopes, and cards;
- shields, levers, pulleys, tiny cranes, simple switches, and small hand-built machines;
- source cards, license tags, review marks, path labels, and attribution tags.

Keep the pool sparse. Objects stand for article concepts: judgment, review, handoff, hidden coupling, failure mode, decision gate, framing, sequencing, prioritization, trust, source context, or conceptual repair.

Linux-flavored props, such as a small terminal shape, a tiny source tag, a signpost, or an attribution card, may appear only as low-key article metaphors while Tux stays the actor. They never become readable screens, logos, badges, or campaign graphics.

## Originality

- Invent the scene from the current article idea, then choose the Tux action that makes it readable. Source facts set identity and boundaries; they are not scene content.
- Before repeating an earlier composition, change the main object; before repeating an action, change Tux's action.
- Keep metaphors low-tech, sparse, and physical.
- Never copy a reference pose or an earlier composition, and use nothing from other archetypes.
