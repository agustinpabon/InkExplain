# Littlebox Motion and Acting

How Littlebox moves, and how the shared structure types from `references/workflow/composition-grid.md` look with Littlebox: each image takes one of the composition families below, with one core idea, one Littlebox action, and the box closed throughout.

## Actions

Collect, catch, hold, carry; close, seal, stamp; sort, fold, compress, bundle; bridge, block, steer, redirect; hide, reveal, store, deliver; spill, overflow, recover, repair.

Littlebox acts through its twig arms and external slips, stacks, parcels, stamps, tags, paths, slots, or props. The body never opens or stretches; arms, paths, arrows, and props may:

- compression: a press plate, heavy slip, squeezed sheet, or output parcel;
- redirection or handoff: an external amber path;
- bridging: Littlebox holds or places a bridge object;
- more targets than two arms: external trays, slots, tags, falling slips, stacks, or parcels, never extra limbs.

### Poses and acting

- Effort lives in the twig arms, the tiny legs, and the props: both arms bracing an external pile, one arm stamping while the other holds a slip, legs planted under a load or mid-step on a curved path.
- The box itself stays compact, closed, and in front-left three-quarter view in every pose; it never bends, opens, or stretches to act.
- Deadpan acting: the dot eyes stay calm whatever happens. The humor is a small closed box doing a serious job.
- Point the arms and the box's position toward the action, so the eye travels from Littlebox to the object it is changing.

### Timing

- A single image shows the decisive moment: the tape pressed down on a sealed bundle, the stamp landing, the folded parcel leaving the twig arm, not the setup or the aftermath.
- Mini comics use two or three beats at most, one action each, with Littlebox closed and consistent across panels: the same proportions, view, eyes, arms, and tape.

## Patterns

- **Intake and packaging** (raw notes, messy content, idea capture, research input, knowledge organization): from one side of the canvas, Littlebox gathers loose slips, blocks, or signals into an external stack, sealed bundle, or labeled packet.
- **Before and after** (chaos to structure, manual to system, vague to clear, scattered to packaged): loose and unstable on the left, compact and calm on the right; Littlebox makes the change in the center with arms, stamps, straps, paths, or a separate press or slot.
- **Handoff path** (workflows, customer journeys, publishing pipelines, sales handoffs, review loops, assistant workflows): one loose curved amber path, not a straight process chart; Littlebox carries, seals, stamps, or steers the object at the handoff point.
- **Hidden interior** (black boxes, model behavior, assumptions, hidden costs, behind-the-scenes systems): show the hidden behavior outside the box with slips, tags, peephole labels, orbiting clues, or small props. A cutaway fails.
- **Pressure and overflow** (bottlenecks, too many tools, overloaded context, queue problems, attention debt, weak systems): Littlebox braces, ties, blocks, catches, or carries an external pile, lid shut under pressure; coral marks only the risk point.
- **Evidence bundle** (trust, proof, documentation, decision records, validation): Littlebox gathers evidence slips and seals one usable external package; coral can mark the final stamp.
- **Mini comic** (failure-to-fix sequences, dry operational humor): two or three loose panels at most, one action each, Littlebox closed and consistent throughout.

### Metaphor invention

Turn the idea into a physical box action:

- scattered inputs: gather them into an external stack;
- unclear decision: sort pieces into labeled piles;
- fragile trust: seal an evidence package;
- broken handoff: a sealed package snags on a curved path;
- over-automation: brace an external spill of tiny tools;
- context compression: fold a large sheet into a small parcel;
- publishing: stamp or hand off one clean signal.

### Layout and flow

- Spread elements across both axes: one enlarged subject, small supporting elements at different heights.
- Prefer one curved amber `#F59E0B` path to several straight arrows. Flow marks stay thinner or lighter than Littlebox's outline and apart from its tape; black arrows appear only as tiny hand-motion marks.

## Supporting objects

Littlebox plus two to four supporting elements. Add a low-tech object only when it clarifies the idea: slip, tape, stamp, shelf, path, slot, scale, tag, latch, handle, tiny stack, or parcel.

## Originality

- The shared originality rules in `references/workflow/composition-grid.md` apply.
- Vary the action across a set: sealing, stamping, carrying, sorting external slips, tying a bundle, blocking a path, handing off a parcel, folding a sheet, bracing a pile, redirecting a curved path.
- Change the surrounding objects before deforming Littlebox; keep the front-left three-quarter box readable.
