# Go Gopher Style

Sparse article sketches in which the Go gopher performs one cognitive action inside an original physical metaphor.

## Canvas

- 16:9 horizontal article illustration by default.
- White or very light background with generous whitespace. No paper texture, gradient, or shadow.
- One core idea and one enlarged action subject per image. The gopher's action takes about 35–55% of the canvas, with empty space around it so the metaphor reads at a glance.

## Linework

- Rough black hand-drawn outlines with visible human looseness.
- The gopher is drawn in the same loose line, simplified for article explanation, and keeps its black outline and white sticker-like border. Its marker set matters more than rendering detail.
- No polished vector finish, fur or texture rendering, or 3D.

## Color

- Black: main strokes, objects, the gopher's outline, and labels.
- Gopher blue for the body; beige for the muzzle and paws.
- Restrained red, orange, or blue accents only for motion, stamps, warning marks, source tags, or decisive emphasis.
- No other fills, no gradients, no shadows.

## Labels

- Usually two to six short labels, copied exactly in the user's language.
- Rough handwritten text, never clean digital type.
- Keep labels clear of the gopher's ears, eyes, nose, teeth, muzzle, paws, outline, and sticker border.
- No titles, top-left headings, or dense text blocks.

## Avoid

- Dense PPT-like diagrams, formal diagrams, and crowded nodes.
- UI screenshots, IDE or terminal scenes, and code screenshots.
- Poster layouts, polished mascot-poster rendering, mascot asset sheets, language posters, and programming-ad framing.
- The Go logo or any logo-forward composition.
- Top-left title artifacts, clean digital labels, and dense text.
