# Go Gopher Motion and Acting

How the Go gopher moves, and how the shared structure types from `references/workflow/composition-grid.md` look with the Go gopher: one core idea, one physical metaphor, and one gopher action per image.

## Actions

Verbs: inspect, point, carry, sort, bridge, repair, guide, stamp, mark, tune, shield, weigh, connect, untangle, map, compare, lift, assemble, steer. Keep every action physical and readable.

- **Inspect** a map, lens, thread, note, gate, or evidence shelf.
- **Pointing paw**: point to the useful turn, risky gap, stable claim, or next action.
- **Carrying clue**: carry a key, envelope, lamp, stamp, map piece, or bridge part.
- **Sorting table**: sort envelopes, cards, buckets, labels, paths, or small evidence pieces.
- **Bridge repair**: bridge two stepping stones, shelves, paths, article sections, or trust gaps; repair a broken bridge, knot, lever, trail, or small hand-built machine.
- **Guide** a reader through a trail, map, gate, or signpost sequence.
- **Stamp** a reviewed idea, checkpoint, source note, or handoff.
- **Path marker**: map a hidden path, dependency, trail, or sequence; mark the decisive point, useful turn, stable claim, or review boundary; steer a reader, signal, claim, or handoff through the decisive path.
- **Shield stance**: shield a fragile idea, boundary, source note, or decision.
- **Scale check**: weigh two claims, tradeoffs, paths, or choices; compare two trails, gates, buckets, shelves, or labels.
- **Thread untangle**: connect threads, trails, shelves, handoffs, or stepping stones; untangle knots, threads, paths, or competing claims.
- **Lift and assemble**: lift a blocked idea, heavy claim, gate, or bridge piece; assemble a small machine, shelf, bridge, path, or review ladder; tune a scale, lamp, compass, signal, lever, or small machine.

### Poses and acting

- Start from the friendly standing posture: upright, compact, and planted, then turn the whole body toward the action.
- Tie the eye direction, paw position, muzzle and teeth orientation, body direction, and standing posture to the action: eyes on the object, paws on it.
- Use the stances the actions name: a pointing paw stretched toward the useful turn, a shield stance planted in front of a fragile idea, both paws on a scale to weigh two claims, the whole body under a bridge piece it lifts.
- Show effort through the lean of the body and the paws, never by stretching the compact limbs or changing the proportions.

### Timing

- A single image shows the decisive moment: the stamp landing on the checkpoint, the last stepping stone set in place, the paw on the useful turn; not the setup or the aftermath.
- Mini comics use two or three beats, one gopher action each, with the gopher identical across panels. State change images give each state its own single action.
- One gopher per image. A handoff, a comparison, or a state change may show the same gopher once per state (two to four at most), each identical in design; never a crowd.

## Patterns

- **Workflow**: input-to-output reasoning, editorial handoffs, review loops, learning sequences, decision paths, and repair flows. Draw a loose left-to-right or curved path; the gopher operates the decisive handoff point: pointing to a turn, carrying a key, sorting envelopes, bridging stepping stones, repairing a broken link, stamping a checkpoint, or steering a small signal through the path.
- **System close-up**: filters, decision points, hidden dependencies, evidence clusters, priority shelves, comparison layers, and resilience checks. Draw only three to five core parts; the gopher works inside the decisive one: inspecting a lens, untangling threads, weighing claims on scales, shielding a fragile idea, tuning a lamp, repairing a lever, or connecting two shelves.
- **Before and after**: vague to clear, tangled to readable, scattered to sorted, fragile to stable, overloaded to focused, stuck to moving. The left side is unstable, the right side calmer; the gopher performs the change in the center with a knot, bridge, map, gate, shelf, scale, stamp, bucket, path marker, or small hand-built machine.
- **State change**: focused review, careful comparison, overloaded sorting, guarded judgment, stuck reasoning, and confident handoff. Two to four compact gopher states, each with one short label and one physical action.
- **Concept metaphor**: judgment scales, trust bridges, decision gates, tangled threads, map trails, evidence shelves, review lamps, hidden keys, routing switches, and small strange machines. Build one memorable low-tech object or hand-built machine and make the gopher its operator.
- **Layers**: layered methods, capability stacks, reasoning ladders, evidence shelves, review depth, confidence building, and staged handoffs. Draw stacked boxes, shelves, steps, or loose construction layers; the gopher carries, shields, stamps, sorts, tunes, repairs, weighs, lifts, or assembles one layer so the method becomes usable.
- **Route map**: idea paths, reader journeys, publishing paths, migration paths, decision trails, and problem-resolution paths. Draw one curved path with a few nodes; the gopher walks, guides, bridges, reroutes, repairs, stamps, marks, or carries something along the decisive segment.
- **Mini comic**: failure-to-fix sequences, repeated reasoning mistakes, handoff stories, comparison lessons, and small process stories. Two or three loose panels, one gopher action each, with the gopher identical across panels.

## Supporting objects

Small, physical objects that make article logic visible:

- maps, folded trail maps, dotted paths, and signposts;
- stepping stones, bridges, gates, shelves, and ladders;
- knots, threads, ropes, hooks, clips, and loose wires;
- lamps, lenses, compasses, scales, stamps, keys, buckets, envelopes, and cards;
- shields, levers, pulleys, tiny cranes, simple switches, and hand-built machines;
- small notes, source tags, checklist tags, review marks, and path labels;
- trails, trail markers, paper paths, split paths, and handoff boards.

Use three to six per image. They stand for judgment, review, sequencing, handoff, hidden coupling, failure modes, decision gates, framing, prioritization, trust, and conceptual repair.

Go-themed cues are allowed only as low-key context that keeps the gopher as the character: a tiny source tag, a signpost, or a blue and beige color echo. Never the Go logo, a logo-forward layout, a language poster, or programming-ad framing.

## Originality

- Invent the scene from the current article idea, then choose the gopher action that makes it readable.
- Use the source facts for identity and credit only, never as scene content: no source-poster or programming-language ad layouts.
- Never reuse the front-facing pose or centered layout of `gopher.png`; draw a new pose for every scene.
- Change the main object before repeating an earlier composition, and change the gopher's action before repeating an earlier action.
- Keep metaphors low-tech, sparse, and physical.
