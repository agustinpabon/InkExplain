# OpenClaw Identity

OpenClaw is the red logo mascot of the OpenClaw project, adapted as a precise, useful, readable article character. In every image of this archetype it is the active operator of the article concept. It suits ideas about review, sorting, connection, boundary setting, comparison, repair, decision gates, and handoffs.

## Identity

Preserve all six markers together, simplified for an article sketch:

- red round body;
- side claw-like arms;
- two antennae;
- black eyes;
- cyan pupils;
- short legs.

This marker list is the identity authority (see `rights.md`). Keep enough of each marker that OpenClaw reads as the logo character while the scene stays sparse.

## Series lock

- **Locked** in a series: all six markers: the red round body, the side claw-like arms, the two antennae, the black eyes, the cyan pupils, and the short legs.
- **Free**: the pose, how the claws grip, which way it faces, its scale, and the objects it handles.
- **Rejected**: a missing marker, a different color or number of claws or antennae, a generic robot or red blob, and a logo-poster look.

## Recognition

- All six markers appear in every image: red round body, side claw-like arms, two antennae, black eyes, cyan pupils, and short legs.
- OpenClaw looks the same across a series and across mini-comic panels.
- OpenClaw sits inside the action, never in a corner as a spectator.
- The round red body anchors the composition without becoming a poster logo.

## Failure modes

- Generic red mascot drift: a generic red character, plush toy, logo blob, or vague red silhouette.
- Generic robot drift, or a different mascot.
- A missing marker: no red round body, missing claws, missing antennae, missing black eyes, missing cyan pupils, or missing short legs.
- An over-rendered toy or a logo icon instead of a sketch.
- Product-poster drift: OpenClaw shown off as a brand mascot in a product poster, launch graphic, logo showcase, UI promotion, or repository banner instead of doing article work.
- Passive placement: OpenClaw is tiny, decorative, or only reacting, or stands beside the concept while props, arrows, or labels carry the meaning.

## Participation test

Imagine OpenClaw removed. If the metaphor still works, OpenClaw is decoration: rewrite the prompt so OpenClaw performs the decisive action.
