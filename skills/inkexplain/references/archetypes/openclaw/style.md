# OpenClaw Style

Sparse hand-drawn article sketches in which the red OpenClaw mascot performs one cognitive action inside an original article metaphor. Identity matters more than rendering detail: keep the drawing simple and OpenClaw unmistakable.

## Canvas

- 16:9 horizontal article illustration by default.
- Clean white or near-white background with generous whitespace.
- One core idea in a low-density composition, with empty space around OpenClaw's action so the metaphor reads at a glance.

## Linework

- Loose black linework with visible hand-drawn looseness.
- Simplified shapes: OpenClaw stays recognizable inside the sketch style without becoming an over-rendered toy or a logo icon.

## Color

- Black: line art, eyes, supporting objects, and labels.
- Red: restrained, tied to OpenClaw's round body and claws.
- Cyan: restrained, tied to the pupils or one small action emphasis, such as a scan line or a marked turn.

## Labels

- Two to six short handwritten labels, copied exactly in the user's language.
- Keep labels clear of OpenClaw's face, antennae, claws, pupils, and legs.
- No full-sentence annotations, dense text blocks, bilingual clutter, or clean digital typography.

## Avoid

- Product posters, launch graphics, logo-poster layouts, and brand ads.
- UI screenshots, IDE or terminal scenes, and code screenshots.
- Formal diagrams, dense PPT-like infographics, and poster layouts.
- Top-left titles and title cards.
