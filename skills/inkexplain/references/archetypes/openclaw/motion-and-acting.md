# OpenClaw Motion and Acting

How OpenClaw moves, and how the shared structure types from `references/workflow/composition-grid.md` look with OpenClaw: one structure, one physical article metaphor, and one active OpenClaw action per image.

## Actions

OpenClaw performs the central cognitive action with a physical, readable verb:

| Verb | What OpenClaw acts on |
|---|---|
| Inspect | a map, lens, thread, note, gate, or evidence shelf |
| Grip | a loose claim, broken bridge, decision key, thread, or risky handoff |
| Sort | envelopes, cards, buckets, labels, paths, or small evidence pieces |
| Bridge | two stepping stones, shelves, paths, article sections, or a trust gap |
| Tune | a scale, lamp, compass, signal, lever, or small machine |
| Stamp | a reviewed idea, checkpoint, source note, or handoff |
| Carry | a key, envelope, lamp, stamp, map piece, or bridge part |
| Map | a hidden path, dependency, trail, or sequence |
| Connect | threads, trails, shelves, handoffs, or stepping stones |
| Shield | a fragile idea, boundary, source note, or decision |
| Compare | two trails, gates, buckets, shelves, or labels |
| Mark | the useful turn, risky gap, stable claim, or next action |
| Untangle | knots, threads, tangled paths, or competing claims |
| Weigh | two claims, trade-offs, paths, or choices on a scale |
| Guide | the reader through a trail, map, gate, or signpost sequence |
| Assemble | a small machine, shelf, bridge, path, or review ladder |
| Repair | a broken bridge, knot, lever, trail, or small hand-built machine |

Choose the verb that is the idea; the action always serves the core idea.

### Poses and acting

Let the body carry the action:

- **Claw grip**: one claw grips a loose idea, thread, key, or bridge piece to show control.
- **Two-claw bridge**: both claw-like arms hold two parts together to show handoff, trust, or repair.
- **Antenna scan**: the antennae lean toward a hidden signal, risky gap, or source note.
- **Cyan focus**: the cyan pupils point straight at the decision point, evidence, or path turn.
- **Short-leg movement**: the short legs walk, climb, or brace beside the decisive object.
- **Red body anchor**: the round red body leans into the action and anchors the composition without becoming a poster logo.

Claws, antennae, pupils, legs, and body direction all point at the same object, so OpenClaw's state (focused, inspecting, gripping, shielding, weighing) reads at a glance.

### Timing

- A single image shows the decisive moment: the claw closing on the thread, the stamp landing on the checkpoint, the two claws pressing the bridge parts together.
- Mini comics use two or three beats, one OpenClaw action each, with all six markers and the same proportions in every panel.

## Patterns

- **Workflow** (handoffs, review loops, learning sequences, decision paths, repair flows): a loose left-to-right or curved path. OpenClaw operates the decisive handoff point: gripping a loose thread, connecting stepping stones, stamping a checkpoint, repairing a bridge, sorting envelopes, or marking the useful turn.
- **System close-up** (filters, decision points, hidden dependencies, evidence clusters, priority shelves): three to five core parts. OpenClaw works inside the decisive one: inspecting a lens, untangling threads, weighing claims, shielding a fragile idea, tuning a lamp, or repairing a lever.
- **Before and after** (vague to clear, tangled to readable, scattered to sorted, fragile to stable, stuck to moving): unstable on the left, calmer on the right. OpenClaw makes the change in the center with its claws and one object: a knot, bridge, map, gate, shelf, scale, stamp, or small hand-built machine.
- **State change** (focused review, careful comparison, overloaded sorting, guarded judgment, stuck reasoning, confident handoff): two to four compact OpenClaw states, each with one short label and one physical action.
- **Concept metaphor** (judgment scales, trust bridges, decision gates, tangled threads, evidence shelves, review lamps, hidden keys): one memorable low-tech object or small strange machine operated by OpenClaw, with claws, antennae, pupils, legs, and body direction all tied to the action.
- **Layers** (layered methods, capability stacks, reasoning ladders, review depth, confidence building): stacked boxes, shelves, steps, or loose construction layers. OpenClaw carries, shields, stamps, tunes, or repairs one layer so the method becomes usable.
- **Route map** (idea paths, reader journeys, publishing or migration paths, decision trails): one curved path with a few nodes. OpenClaw walks, guides, bridges, reroutes, marks, or maps the decisive segment.
- **Mini comic** (failure-to-fix sequences, repeated reasoning mistakes, handoff stories, comparison lessons): two or three loose panels with one OpenClaw action each.

## Supporting objects

Small, physical objects that make article logic visible:

- maps, folded maps, dotted paths, trails, and signposts;
- stepping stones, bridges, gates, shelves, and ladders;
- knots, threads, ropes, hooks, claws, clips, and loose wires;
- lamps, lenses, compasses, scales, stamps, keys, buckets, envelopes, and cards;
- shields, levers, pulleys, tiny cranes, simple switches, and small hand-built machines;
- small notes, source tags, checklist tags, and review marks.

They stand for judgment, review, sequencing, handoff, hidden coupling, failure modes, decision gates, framing, prioritization, and conceptual repair. Keep the scene sparse: three to six supporting objects in most images.

## Originality

- Invent the scene from the current article idea, then choose the OpenClaw action that makes it readable.
- Use the source and logo facts for identity only. Never copy official OpenClaw screenshots, product UI, logo-poster layouts, or repository visuals, and never trace a pose from a source image.
- Before repeating an earlier composition, change the main object; before repeating an action, change OpenClaw's action.
- The approved gallery samples (see `rights.md`) calibrate density and attitude only. Do not reproduce their scenes (two breakpoints, sorting by purpose, one fish with many uses, a handoff path, an information well, an idea press, content fermentation, a trust bridge) unless the user asks for that specific image.
