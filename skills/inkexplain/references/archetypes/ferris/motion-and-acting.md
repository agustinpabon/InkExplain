# Ferris Motion and Acting

How Ferris moves, and how the shared structure types from `references/workflow/composition-grid.md` look with Ferris: one core idea, one physical metaphor, and one active Ferris action per image.

## Actions

Give Ferris the central action:

- clamp a decision gate, or guard a compiler gate;
- sort ownership tags, or compile scattered slips into one stack;
- borrow a rope and return it;
- stamp a type-check, proof, or review result;
- carry a crate stack, or bridge a dependency gap;
- untangle dependency knots;
- lock a release gate, or release a finished handoff;
- inspect a safety net, reroute a risky path, or balance competing constraints.

Useful verbs: clamp, sort, guard, borrow, return, compile, type-check, stamp, carry, bridge, untangle, lock, inspect, reroute, balance, release. The action always serves the core idea.

### Poses and acting

- Body language: focused, guarding, sorting, type-checking, borrowing, compiling, inspecting, untangling, bracing, stamping, or releasing.
- Point the whole crab at the action: claws on the object, eyes and shell angle aimed at it, legs set under the load.
- Let the claws do the work: one claw clamped on a compiler gate, both claws pulling a dependency knot apart, a claw bringing a type-check stamp down.
- Show effort through the body: legs braced wide while balancing a safety net between two crate stacks, the shell tilted forward while carrying a crate stack across a gap.

### Timing

- A single image shows the decisive moment: the claw closing on the gate, the stamp landing on the type-check slip, the release lock opening; not the setup or the aftermath.
- Mini comics use two or three beats, one Ferris action each, with Ferris drawn the same way in every panel.

## Patterns

- **Workflow** (review loops, build pipelines, trust handoffs, safety checks, dependency movement, release paths): a loose left-to-right or curved path. Ferris operates the decisive handoff point: clamping a compiler gate, sorting ownership tags, stamping a type-check slip, carrying a crate stack, or opening a release lock.
- **System close-up** (filters, validation modules, decision points, hidden costs, dependency clusters, safety layers): only three to five core parts. Ferris works inside the decisive one: guarding a compiler gate, inspecting a safety net, untangling dependency knots, balancing a lifetime clock, or rerouting a risky path.
- **Before and after** (unsafe to safe, tangled to clear, unreviewed to ready, fragile to resilient, vague to typed): unstable on the left, calmer on the right. Ferris performs the change in the center with a borrow rope, type-check stamp, safety net, review gate, crate stack, or release lock.
- **State change** (focused review, defensive safety posture, overloaded dependency work, careful handoff, failure-mode anxiety, release confidence): two to four compact Ferris states, each with one short label and one physical action.
- **Concept metaphor** (trust machines, proof gates, borrow bridges, lifetime clocks, review locks, resilience paths): one memorable low-tech object or small strange machine, operated by Ferris with claws and body direction tied to the action.
- **Layers** (capability stacks, verification layers, dependency layers, release readiness, review depth): stacked boxes, shelves, steps, or loose construction layers. Ferris carries, locks, stamps, clamps, sorts, or balances one layer so the method becomes usable.
- **Route map** (learning, launch, publishing, migration, decision, and problem-resolution paths): one curved path with a few nodes. Ferris walks, guards, bridges, reroutes, clamps, or releases the decisive segment.
- **Mini comic** (failure-to-fix sequences, repeated dependency mistakes, unsafe shortcuts, review humor): two or three loose panels, one Ferris action per panel, with Ferris consistent across panels.

## Supporting objects

Low-tech, Rust-themed props: ownership tags, borrow ropes, lifetime clocks, compiler gates, type-check stamps, safety nets, unsafe warning flags, trait stamps, dependency knots, crate stacks, release locks, and review gates.

- Use one or two per image; an extra object must make Ferris's action clearer.
- They are metaphors for general article concepts: trust, proof, safety, review, dependency, handoff, hidden cost, failure mode, decision gate, and system resilience.
- Draw them as plain objects, with no logos, marks, or official badges.

## Originality

Invent every scene from the current article:

1. Turn the abstract idea into a physical action Ferris can perform: clamp, sort, guard, borrow, return, compile, type-check, stamp, carry, bridge, untangle, lock, inspect, reroute, balance, or release.
2. Choose one or two supporting objects that make the action visible.
3. Make Ferris the central operator.

Examples of the method, not scenes to reuse:

- Weak proof: Ferris clamps a compiler gate while sorting evidence slips.
- Unsafe shortcut: Ferris plants an unsafe warning flag beside a borrow-rope bridge.
- Tangled dependency: Ferris untangles a dependency knot around crate stacks.
- Trust handoff: Ferris stamps a type-check slip before a release lock opens.
- Hidden cost: Ferris inspects a lifetime clock behind a review gate.
- Brittle system: Ferris balances a safety net between two crate stacks.
- Delayed release: Ferris guards a release lock while checking a review gate.

Across a set, vary Ferris's action and change the low-tech object before changing any identity cue. Never trace, pose-match, or recreate artwork from rustacean.net, public mascot asset sheets, or official logo arrangements; draw a new pose for each scene.
